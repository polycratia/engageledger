# engageledger

Keep the money terms of a contracting engagement as a signed append-only journal: price moves only by change orders signed by both parties, acceptance is a business-day clock stopped only by a rejection that names a criterion, and invoices are computed from the journal.

The journal is JSONL where each line carries the SHA-256 of the line before it, so a replay either reaches the end or names the first line it refuses; the chain is plumbing under the fold that prices the engagement.

`terms.Fold` is that fold: it reads the events into the current milestones, the total price and the time-and-materials cap, all in minor units of the one contract currency. A number moves only when `change_order.approved` events from both the client and the contractor name the hash of the line the proposal was written on; proposed, declined and withdrawn change orders are listed next to the terms they left untouched. The fold names the first event it refuses, among them a change order naming an unknown milestone, one priced in another currency, one that removes or reprices an accepted milestone, and a second decision on a settled change order.

An approval carries its evidence: `approval.Approval{party, method, evidence_hash, signature}`. An `ed25519` approval signs the canonical JSON of the proposal body and verifies against the key `contract.opened` lists for the party, so the journal alone can check it; a `recorded` approval - an email or an e-sign export kept outside the journal - leaves behind only the SHA-256 of that evidence, which nothing in the journal can tie to the body it stands for. The fold refuses a bad signature, an approval by someone who is not a party, a party the contract head lists no key for, and an approval naming a body hash other than the one it approves. Every effective change order reports the strength of its evidence - `signed`, `recorded`, or `asserted` where an approval carried no evidence at all - and `Terms.WeakTotal` adds up the money that moved on anything weaker than a signature.

`acceptance.Fold` reads the same journal into the acceptance state of every milestone: `delivered` while its window runs, `accepted`, `rejected`, and `deemed_accepted` when the window runs out. A `milestone.rejected` event must name at least one criterion of that milestone, each with a non-empty defect note; a rejection that names none fails no criterion, so the fold lists it among `Refused` and the milestone stays `delivered` with its clock running. A valid rejection closes the window, and the next `milestone.delivered` opens a retest window that restarts on the full term or resumes the days the closed one had left, as the `retest_window` of the contract head asks. Any event on an accepted or deemed-accepted milestone is refused.

That clock is `bizday.Calendar{version, zone, weekend, holidays, close}`: the zone the client counts days in, the weekdays that are weekend, the holidays the contract lists and the local time a business day ends. `Calendar.Hash` is the canonical SHA-256 a contract head carries, so a replay can tell which calendar a deadline was computed on, and two heads that spell the same calendar differently hash the same. `Calendar.Deadline(deliveredAt, n)` is the close of the n-th business day after a delivery, returned as one UTC instant: a delivery past close counts from the next business day, weekends and listed holidays are skipped, and the wall-clock close maps to another instant on either side of a daylight-saving transition. The zoneinfo database is embedded, so the same journal reads the same deadline on any host.

A delivery fixes its deadline once: `acceptance.Deliver` writes a `milestone.delivered` event carrying that UTC instant as `deadline_utc` next to the `calendar_hash` it was computed on, and the fold takes the instant as recorded. `Acceptance.Verify` is what checks it: it recomputes every recorded deadline with the calendar version whose hash the delivery names and returns a `Drift{seq, milestone, recorded, recomputed, cause}` for each one that comes out elsewhere - `tzdata` where the day the window closes on holds and only the instant of that close moves, `calendar` where the day itself moves - without writing any of them back, so neither a zoneinfo update nor a correction shortens a window that is already running. `calendar.revised` appends another calendar version with its own hash, which the deliveries recorded after it count on, while the windows opened before it keep both the version they named and the deadline they recorded; a delivery naming a calendar the journal never appended, or one a revision superseded, is refused. `Deem` then moves a milestone to `deemed_accepted` only once the recorded instant has passed.

A `hold.proposed` event names a pause of one milestone between two dates, and a `hold.approved` event carries one party's approval of that proposal, evidenced as the approval of a change order is. A hold pauses nothing until every party has approved it: `Acceptance.Holds` lists a hold one side approved next to the effective ones, and only the approval that leaves no party without one makes it effective. The fold refuses a hold that overlaps an effective hold of the same milestone - whether the overlap arrives with the proposal or with the approval that would make it effective - one starting after the recorded deadline of the window has passed, one on a milestone with no open window, a second approval by the same party, and an approval of a hold no `hold.proposed` before it carries.

`nte.Fold` reads the not-to-exceed cap and the time logged against it: a `time.logged` event carries the `minutes` worked and the `date` they were worked on, and the cap is the `nte_cap_minutes` of the contract head, raised only by a change order carrying a `set_nte_cap` op that every party approved, which counts the cap version up. An entry that would carry the logged minutes past the cap in force is refused in full, with an error naming the minutes that remain: nothing shortens an entry to make it fit, and a head that names no cap leaves no minutes to log. `Notify` appends the notices due at 75% and 90% of the cap as `nte.notice` events keyed by the SHA-256 of the contract, the cap version and the threshold, so a replay or a second run appends no notice twice, while a raised cap re-arms both thresholds under its new version.
